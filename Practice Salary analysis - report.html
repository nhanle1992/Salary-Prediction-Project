Salary Prediction Model: Business Report

1. Introduction & Objectives

Problem Statement

The primary problem addressed is the accurate prediction of an individual's salary based on their years of experience.

Summary Objective

To build a robust, interpretable, and generalizable machine learning model that accurately predicts salary based on years of professional experience.

2. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) Insights

Years of Experience Distribution

The 'YearsExperience' variable shows a relatively even distribution, ranging from 1.2 to 10.6 years, with no significant outliers. This indicates a good spread of experience levels in the dataset.

Salary Distribution

The 'Salary' variable exhibits a right-skewed distribution, with salaries ranging from $37,732 to $122,392. This indicates more individuals earning lower salaries and fewer earning very high salaries, but no extreme outliers.

Relationship between Years of Experience and Salary

A very strong positive linear relationship exists between 'YearsExperience' and 'Salary', with a Pearson Correlation Coefficient of approximately 0.9782. This suggests that as years of experience increase, salary tends to increase proportionally.

3. Data Pre-processing Steps

  1. **Data Loading & Initial Checks:** Loaded `Salary_dataset.csv`, confirmed no missing values or duplicate rows.
  2. **Data Cleaning:** Removed redundant 'Unnamed: 0' column if present.
  3. **Feature Scaling (Standardization):** Applied StandardScaler to 'YearsExperience' after train-test split to ensure features contribute equally.
  4. **Train-Test Split:** Divided data into 80% training and 20% testing sets (using `random_state=42`) for robust model evaluation.

4. Model Building & Comparison

A comparison between a Simple Linear Regression model and a Polynomial Regression model (degree 2) using 5-fold cross-validation was performed. - **Simple Linear Regression:** Average cross-validated RMSE = $5694.82, Average R2 = 0.9224. - **Polynomial Regression (Degree 2):** Average cross-validated RMSE = $6040.71, Average R2 = 0.9143. The simple linear regression model was selected as the polynomial model's average RMSE was not meaningfully lower (less than 1% improvement) than the simple model's. The simpler model is preferred for its interpretability and lower risk of overfitting.

5. Modeling Results & Evaluation

The final selected model is the **Simple Linear Regression**. * **Model Intercept:** $74208.62 (Estimated base salary when YearsExperience is zero after scaling). * **Coefficient (YearsExperience):** $27151.54 (For every one-unit increase in scaled 'YearsExperience', salary is expected to increase by this amount). * **Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE) on test set:** $7059.04 (On average, predictions are off by this amount, which is reasonable given the salary range). * **R-squared (R2 Score) on test set:** 0.9024 (Approximately 90.24% of salary variation is explained by YearsExperience).

Diagnostic Visuals

The **Actual vs. Predicted Plot** shows a strong alignment between actual and predicted salaries, with points clustering near the 45-degree line, indicating good predictive capability.

The **Residual Plot** displays residuals randomly scattered around zero, confirming the linearity assumption and suggesting that the model does not have systemic biases across the prediction range. No clear patterns like funnel shapes or curves were observed, reinforcing the model's suitability.

6. Conclusion & Key Findings

Summary of Key Findings:

  • There is a very strong positive linear relationship between years of experience and salary.
  • The simple linear regression model provides a highly effective and interpretable solution for salary prediction, explaining over 90% of the variance in salary for this dataset.
  • The model's performance was validated using cross-validation and diagnostic plots, confirming its reliability and suitability for the given data.
  • The average prediction error (RMSE) for the chosen model is approximately $7059.04, which is a good result given the salary range of the dataset.

In conclusion, the developed simple linear regression model effectively predicts salary based on years of experience, offering a balance between accuracy and interpretability. The rigorous validation process confirms its reliability for this specific dataset.
